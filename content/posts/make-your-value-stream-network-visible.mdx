## The Hidden Plumbing of Work

Every organization has value streams — the invisible plumbing through which ideas become outcomes.  
They already exist; most teams simply can't *see* them.

![Hidden Plumbing](/hidden-plumbing.png)

### The Value Perception Gap

**Customer Reality:** "I asked for something and got it in five days"  
**Internal Reality:** 14-day stories, 52-day epics, and 120-day features

Our delivery systems are optimized locally, but **value flows globally**.

---

## The Illusion of Local Efficiency

Metrics like DORA can give a false sense of speed — "elite" performers on paper —  
while the system as a whole remains slow.  
When flow is measured only at the story level, the customer's journey remains opaque.

![Local Efficiency](/local_efficiency.png)

> ⚠️ **The Problem**  
> What's missing isn't more metrics; it's **systemic visibility**.

---

## The Disassembly and Reassembly of Work

Modern software delivery resembles manufacturing,  
but instead of visible parts, we have invisible ones — stories, APIs, handoffs, approvals.  
Each new initiative disassembles and reassembles work across dozens of teams.

![Disassembly Process](/disassembly.png)

This constant recombination creates what I call the **Value Stream Network** —  
a living web of dependencies that defines how value actually moves.

---

## Enter Graph Theory

If we think in **graph terms**, each team, system, or individual is a **node**,  
and every handoff, dependency, or shared artifact is an **edge**.

![Graph Theory Visualization](/graph_theory.png)

Just like airline routes, there are no shortcuts;  
every piece of customer value must traverse this network to reach its destination.

### Graph Theory Tools for Value Streams

- **🎯 Degree centrality** highlights teams overloaded with dependencies  
- **🚧 Betweenness centrality** reveals bottlenecks acting as control points  
- **🏘️ Clusters** show sub-networks (domains, platforms) that can be optimized as one  
- **📏 Path lengths** explain why "everyone is busy, yet delivery is slow"

## Why Static Maps Fail

![Static Maps](/static.png)

Traditional value-stream maps are snapshots — out of date before the ink dries.  
Dependencies change weekly, sometimes daily.  
Entropy always increases; visibility doesn't.

> 🚀 **The Solution**  
> To manage today's complexity, we need **dynamic network views** —  
> always on, data-driven, and automatically refreshed as work moves.

---

## Making Demand vs Capacity Visible

![Demand vs Capacity](/demand.png)

By overlaying dependency counts with flow times,  
we can see where demand outstrips capacity and forecast **projected flow times**.  
This turns planning into a living, adaptive process instead of a quarterly guess.

> 🧭 **The Analogy**  
> It's the difference between **static traffic signs** and **real-time GPS**.

## The Universe Isn't Flat
